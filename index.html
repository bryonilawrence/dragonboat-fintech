dragonboat Overview
🐉
What is dragonboat?
Intuit is replacing City Plan with a new product portfolio management platform called dragonboat — mandatory, company-wide, Go-Live 6/1. dragonboat is a hub to connect teams, align on goals and success metrics, and communicate 6-month portfolio roadmaps. It integrates strategy, intake, planning, and delivery into one platform, replacing the fragmented set of tools we use today.
Since City Plan never fully met Fintech's needs, most of our teams are working out of Smartsheet, Airtable, or Google Sheets today for planning/roadmapping. dragonboat will be the single source of truth for FY27 planning.
dragonboat is the system of record for roadmaps, initiatives, and dependencies
Connected Experience
One tool to manage both roadmaps and dependencies. Experiences are cohesive, connected, intuitive.
Execution Clarity
PMs, TPMs, and PDs save hours of frustration with no more translating between multiple sources of truth.
Connected Execution
Integration with 3&1 Year Planning data, Jira, and PET enables seamless visibility across goals, work, execution progress, and resourcing.
Decision Velocity
Faster, friction-free environment where trusted roadmap views and execution dashboards are produced automatically.
dragonboat is built for IYE, not IYP
Maintaining a living plan of record requires capabilities the old toolchain couldn’t support.
Taxonomy is the foundation
A shared work structure that defines how all work is organized and connected in dragonboat.
Structured dependency management
Dependencies are tied to initiatives and submitted with receiver-specific intake questions, ensuring quality and faster resolution.
Bi-directional Jira sync
Execution updates flow automatically between Jira and dragonboat, keeping roadmaps current without manual reconciliation.
Multi-level views, one source of truth
Portfolio, product, and team views all reflect the same data — no separate decks or reporting needed.
Problems Today (IYP)
  • No shared plan of record — no single source of truth for what is being built and when
  • Churn / long average wait times to resolve inter-team planning requests
  • High priority inflation across folio requests
  • Poor dependency request quality — low signal, high back-and-forth
  • Lack of integration to Jira, meaning low execution traceability
  • Too many planning processes and shadow systems (Google Sheets, Smartsheet, Slack, LeanIX)
What dragonboat Solves (IYE)
  • Single source of truth for roadmaps, goals, workstreams, and dependencies
  • Clear ROI — one place to see what is planned and why
  • Golden thread from Goals → Workstreams → Initiatives → Epics in Jira
  • Structured intake via a customizable dependency request questionnaire — replaces ad-hoc Slack/email requests
  • 10× velocity goal — PDX's stated outcome for the TECH-IG05 transformation

Initiative Management — What You Need to Know

An initiative is the pivot point, bridging strategy and the engineering work that brings the strategy to life. It is a large, outcome-driven body of work that delivers specific customer or business outcomes.

What Every Initiative Needs
A parent Workstream, a named owner, a driving organization, a release quarter, and all four Quad roles assigned.
Why It Matters
An initiative that isn’t properly created cannot be hydrated — and one that isn’t hydrated cannot generate a dependency.
When Would You Create a New Initiative?
Most of your work was defined during 3&1 Yr. Planning and will be brought into dragonboat for hydration. If the scope of your work changed from what was aligned during Annual Planning, you would need to create a new initiative.
What Is The Quad*?
The Quad is the leadership engine of every initiative — the four roles that must be present, assigned, and aligned before any initiative can proceed to hydration, dependency filing, or execution.
PM
Owns the initiative, scope, and roadmap integrity
PD
Owns solution delivery, Epics, and backlog readiness
TPM
Drives execution, dependency management, and risk escalation
Arch
Confirms technical feasibility and sequencing
Hydrating Initiatives — What You Need to Know

Hydration turns a named initiative into an execution-ready commitment. Pre-populated dependency data from annual planning is reviewed and updated to reflect Quad decisions — before any dependency is submitted.

What does “hydration” mean?
Completing an initiative with enough definition to confirm scope, align the Quad, and identify what cross-team support is needed.
What “good” looks like
A defined customer problem, business impact, target timeline, linked PRD, and explicit Quad alignment on solution approach and sequencing.
The gate condition
Hydration is the quality gate before dependencies are filed. Nothing gets submitted until it’s passed.
The Hydration Checklist
A fully hydrated initiative has the following:
✓
Customer Problem: who it solves for, what barrier it removes
✓
Business Impact: measurable outcome of doing (or not doing) the work
✓
Target timeline: start and end dates confirmed by the Quad
✓
PRD or equivalent: linked via “Links” field or “Attach File” tab
✓
Quad alignment: solution, sequencing, and readiness confirmed
✓
Dependencies identified: identify what work requires external team support (if applicable)
Key Takeaways — Initiative Management
1
Initiatives are the center of execution, enabling the IYE Golden Thread from goals to delivery.
2

3

4

5

6

2 — Setting Dependencies
Old Model — IYP
Administrative & Rarely Updated
Dependencies were standalone tickets — often disconnected from the work itself. Low quality, rarely linked to Jira, and almost never updated after submission. The receiver had little context on what was actually needed.
New Model — IYE
Structural Component of an Initiative
A dependency is not a standalone ticket. It is a structural part of an Initiative — created when a PM or TPM breaks an initiative into epics and identifies a capability gap their team cannot fill. Owned by the quad. Reviewed on a quarterly rhythm with clear triage SLAs for in-quarter requests.
🔗
When to create a dependency: When you break an initiative down into epics and identify a capability gap that your own team cannot fill. That gap becomes a dependency. The earlier you flag it, the better — the quarterly review is when accepted dependencies are confirmed for the delivery quarter.
Day 3 — Triage
Receiving team acknowledges the request and assigns an owner.
Day 7 — Align on Solution
Quad-to-quad alignment on whether and how the dependency can be fulfilled.
Day 14 — Response
Formal accept, reject, or redirect. If rejected, the requesting team adjusts the initiative scope.

What Are 6-Quarter Roadmaps & Why They Matter

A roadmap in dragonboat is not a static deck — it’s a living view of the same data you use to manage execution. It updates as your initiatives, priorities, and dependencies evolve.

What Is It?
●
A living plan of record for what we’re building, when, and in what sequence
●
Rolling 6-quarter horizon with high-fidelity near-term + directional outer quarters
●
Updated as priorities, dependencies, and execution realities evolve
●
A single source of truth that aligns teams and enables faster trade-offs
What They Enable (Benefits)
Lighter-weight annual planning
Improved alignment across Product, Capability, and GTM teams
Look-ahead visibility to enable long- and short-term trade-off decisions
Time saved for higher-value work
Stronger portfolio-level focus and capacity alignment
How Roadmaps Work in dragonboat (and Why It's Better)
Generated on demand
Roadmaps are created from the same data used to manage execution. No manual creation or reformatting required.
Built on initiatives
Roadmaps are generated from initiative data — keeping initiatives up to date ensures the roadmap stays accurate.
Produced at different altitudes
The same roadmap can be viewed at portfolio, product, or team level to give each audience the right level of detail from a single source of truth.
Roadmap Views in dragonboat
Gantt Chart View
Timeline bars showing initiative delivery across quarters. Best for sequencing and dependency visualization.
Swimlane View
Team-by-team horizontal lanes. Best for cross-team alignment and capacity discussions.
List View
Tabular view of all initiatives with owner, quarter, status, and goal columns. Best for bulk editing.
3D Dynamic View
Multi-dimensional view across portfolios, goals, and quarters. Best for executive-level briefings.

Dependency Management
Dependencies in dragonboat are not standalone tickets — they are structural parts of initiatives, with a system-supported workflow from submission to execution.
What’s Changing for Receiving Teams

Receiving teams now have a structured, system-supported workflow in dragonboat — from how requests arrive to how they become execution-ready work. Four things are different under IYE:

The Triad
Each receiving team has a defined Triad (Dependency Steward, Product Lead, Engineering Lead). Requests route automatically to the right owners in dragonboat.
Streamlined Communication Between Requester and Receiver
The tool allows for notifications and updated statuses, reducing the back-and-forth between the requester and receiver.
Inbound Dependencies Arrive Stack Ranked and Aligned to Goals and Roadmaps
Inbound dependencies will already include goal alignment, a 1-N priority rank, and alignment to initiatives → workstreams → sub-goals → goals — everything needed for receiving teams to make confident decisions.
Promote Aligned Dependencies to New Epics in Jira from Within dragonboat
Aligned dependencies are promoted to Epics and are parented by the requesting Initiative, enabling real-time progress tracking for the requesting team.
Dependency Workflow — Statuses Explained

Every dependency moves through a defined status lifecycle in dragonboat. Here’s what each status means:

Requester
Draft
Dependency has been created but not yet submitted to the receiving team.
Requester
Submitted
Sent to the receiving team. Awaiting acknowledgment or routing to “In Review.”
Requester
Withdrawn
Requester may “Withdraw” if the initiative is deprioritized or the dependent work is no longer required.
Recipient
Need More Info
Receiving team needs clarification before they can route to review.
Recipient
In Review
Receiving team is actively evaluating the request.
Recipient
Parked
Lower priority than current plan — will be revisited in a subsequent cycle. Either party can set to Parked.
Recipient
Declined
Request was deemed outside the scope of the receiving team’s platform layer and better suited for the application layer.
Recipient
Aligned
Recipient has created or linked one or more Epics. Newly created Epics are parented by the requesting Initiative.
Recipient
Aligned for InnerSource
Epic is focused on review of contributed code changes. For chained dependencies, a new Initiative should be created from the dependency.
Key Takeaways — Dependency Management
1
Each receiving team has a defined Triad (Dependency Steward, Product Lead, Engineering Lead) pre-populated in dragonboat, responsible for solutioning and decisioning inbound dependencies.
2
Prioritization and decisions happen in one system, with full context across requests, timelines, and business goals.
3
Aligned dependencies are promoted to Epics in dragonboat and are parented by the requesting team’s initiative, enabling real-time progress tracking for the requesting team.

Fintech UAT Risk Radar
Based on program documents, early access feedback, and Fintech-specific planning context — here's what we're predicting will surface during Broad UAT.
🔴
P0 — Datalake Not Live During UAT Window
The ODL (datalake) integration is confirmed live June 1, but Broad UAT is running in May. We need our BDA to be able to test the integration between dragonboat and the datalake before the tool goes live, so we can ensure we are able to get work done using the tool.
🔴
P0 — No AI / MCP Connector for Claude
Intuit's mandate is the 3× AI-native builder initiative. But dragonboat currently has no planned AI integration or MCP connector for Claude. Users will immediately ask "where's the AI?" — and there's no answer. This creates a credibility gap between the pitch and the product. Risk: erodes trust in the broader IYE transformation narrative.
🟠
P1 — Batch Upload Overwrites vs. Updates
Confirmed in the Living FAQ: batch uploads add new data but cannot update existing records. Teams migrating initiative data from their own trackers will attempt to "re-upload" to correct mistakes and end up with duplicates. This was flagged in Early Access and 20 feedback items were still "Reviewing" at close — meaning it may not be resolved by Broad UAT. Risk: data integrity issues that undermine confidence in dragonboat as a SOT.
🟠
P1 — Workstream Delegation Feature Gap
The SME deck explicitly flags that "dragonboat has feature gaps (e.g., Workstream Delegation) that must be closed prior to final UAT." Fintech has complex org structures where delegation of planning authority across workstreams is standard. If this feature isn't closed by Broad UAT, PMs running cross-functional initiatives will hit a hard wall. Risk: blocks a core use case for larger Fintech programs.
🟠
P1 — Required Fields Still In Flux
During Early Access, required field configuration was still changing mid-test. If fields are still shifting going into Broad UAT, participants will hit inconsistent validation errors depending on when they log in — and worse, what they test may not reflect what they actually use on 6/1. My concern here is less about the tool and more about our ability to change manage a moving target: if the experience at UAT doesn't match go-live, we lose credibility with the very people we need to be our biggest advocates. Risk: UAT differs from go-live experience, and teams show up on Day 1 feeling misled.
🟠
P1 — Functions Interlock Deferred (Legal, Privacy, Sales)
Enabling Functions (Legal, Privacy, Sales) interlock is explicitly deferred to a future release. Fintech programs with compliance dependencies will discover they can't represent cross-functional gating in dragonboat yet. Risk: The tool gets reported as "not fit for purpose" for regulated work, even when it's on our roadmaps — i.e. the tool does not become SOT for all work.
🟡
P2 — Change Management Cascade Delay
Exec comms cascade was delayed as of 3/27, set to go out 4/30. If comms about leadership alignment haven't been sent before Broad UAT, users will show up to test a tool the broad user population hasn't heard of — generating "why are we doing this?" questions instead of productive feedback. Risk: low engagement quality during UAT due to lack of leadership buy-in context.
🟡
P2 — Input Goals Present at Go-Live But Not Final
Input Goals (IGs) will be loaded into dragonboat by 6/1, but they won't be final versions. Status tracking won't activate until IGs are fully finalized and brought over by PDX. Teams assuming they can start health tracking immediately at go-live will be wrong. Risk: Teams start entering status data against placeholder IGs, creating data integrity issues.
⚑ Confirmed by PDX as a known constraint — not yet widely communicated to dragonboat testers/users.
🟡
P2 — E2E Prioritization Not Available at Go-Live
End-to-end prioritization won't be available on 6/1 — PDX plans to standardize this in a future phase. Teams will need to maintain their current prioritization processes (Google Sheets, Smartsheets, etc.) in the interim. Risk: Immediate "this tool doesn't do what we were told" feedback from users who expected full portfolio prioritization on Day 1.
⚑ Confirmed by PDX — not yet widely communicated to dragonboat testers/users.
🟡
P2 — UAT Scenarios Modeled on Future-State IYE, Not Today's Workflows
PDX's UAT test cases are built around the future-state IYE process — not how teams operate today. Testing theoretical workflows means testers can't validate whether dragonboat actually replaces their current day-to-day tools. Real friction points and edge cases will go undetected, only surfacing post-go-live. Risk: UAT passes with low-quality signal, followed by a wave of "this doesn't work for us" feedback after 6/1.
